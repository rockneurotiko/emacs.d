Kill vs Delete in Meow

The single most-confusing thing in meow. Print this.

The rule: after a selection, the verb is k (meow-kill), not d (meow-delete).

Why d "didn't work"

Meow has two different notions of "what's selected":

Concept What it is How you got it
Meow selection A highlighted range with expansion hints, but Emacs's region-active-p may be nil. w next-word, b back-word, f find, l till, plain h t n p.
Active region Real Emacs region. region-active-p is t. e line, m mark-word, M mark-symbol, o block, the things (, .), and the capital -expand motions H T N P W.

Verbs marked "supports fallback behaviour" in the meow docs (d, c, x) check region-active-p. When it's nil, they fall back to the single-character version.

"meow-kill — Kill current region. This command supports fallback behaviour. Default to meow-C-k."
— meow COMMANDS.org

So w3d deletes one char: the word motion left a meow selection but not an active region, so d fell back to delete-char.

The lookup table

You want… After a selection use For single-char use
Remove + put on kill-ringk meow-kill ✓d meow-delete
Copyx meow-save ✓—
Replace + enter insertc meow-change ✓c falls back to meow-change-char
Comment; meow-comment ✓—
Duplicate (your custom)/ rock--meow-duplicate ✓same key — duplicates char when no region

Two motions that always leave an active region

These are the "safe" motions where d does work, because they create a real Emacs region. Worth knowing because they're the bridge between meow's selection world and Emacs's region world:

  • e meow-line — always selects whole lines as an active region.
  • m meow-mark-word, M meow-mark-symbol — wraps the thing under cursor.
  • , . < > — the "things" family (lesson 0002).
  • Capital movement keys H T N P W B — the -expand variants.

Mnemonic

k for kill the selection.
d for delete one char.
