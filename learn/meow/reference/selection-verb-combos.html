Selection + Verb Combos

Selection is the noun. Verb is what happens to it. Chain them. Print this.

The pattern: build a selection (motion / thing / expansion) then press a verb (k / c / x / ; / / / y / ?). One unit of thought.

The verbs you have

The forgotten verbs

These are verbs you have bound that you almost certainly don't use yet. They each unlock a workflow:

  • j meow-join — joins lines in the selection. Try .pj to collapse a paragraph onto one line.
  • u meow-pop-selection — undoes the last selection change, not the last edit. Saved you many times when you over-expand with a digit.
  • r meow-replace — replaces the selection with the current kill-ring head. x (copy) then later .pr = "replace this paragraph with what I just copied."
  • G meow-grab and R meow-swap-grab — secondary-selection clipboard. For another lesson.

The one rule that ties them all

After every motion, ask yourself: "what verb?" If the answer is "none, I just wanted to move there", press g to cancel the selection. Otherwise, finish the thought with a verb.

One gotcha

For selecting N lines, use eN (meow-line + digit), not .lN. They look similar but they're different selection types — only meow-line is an expand-line selection that grows cleanly with digits. Thing-selection (.l) is for one-shot "select this line as a thing" — not for counting.

Use . + thing-key when the thing has a natural boundary (paragraph p, defun d, string g, parens r/s/c, buffer b). Use expand-* keys (e, m, M, o) when you want to expand by count.
